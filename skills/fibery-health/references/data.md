# Data pass

How the records of the regular databases in scope fill their fields, from the script's facts with `DATA = true`; automations belong to the structure pass. Under about 20 records, give counts ("5 of 7"), not percentages. Over 2,000 records, a database is read from its newest 1,000 (its `sample` says so): numbers marked `all` cover every record, so quote those, and keep "of the newest 1,000" next to the rest.

- **Unused fields**: `fields` filled in 5% of records or fewer (checkbox: never ticked), judged by `all` where it's given; under about 20 records, only fields never filled. Skip formulas, fields marked `new` (added this month), the standard rich-text Description and opt-in flags meant for a few records. A relation is one link seen from two databases: it's in use when `other side filled in` shows the other database links back. Tidy-up, one line per database.
- **Retired fields**: filled in many records overall (`all`) but none of the newest, or `empty on the N newest, since <month>` with N a good share of the records: people stopped using it. Not when the field fills late in a record's life (a leave date, a resolution), was `added` only recently, or is a collection relation whose `newest linked … made` is recent: those links are made from the other side, on its new records. Say so instead of calling it unused, and name what still reads it (`read by`, the `automation` lines) before anyone deletes it. Tidy-up; Important when a formula or rule people rely on still reads it, since its results go stale.
- **Missing links**: `emptyLinks`, a single relation or person field empty in some records, with their split by state or status, and `open`, the unfinished records without it over the whole database. Judge by the split: open records without it matter (an Insight still collecting feedback with no Product Area, a strategic theme in progress with no Owner); finished ones don't, and some records don't need it (an idea without a campaign, a feature in Icebox without a release). Skip a retired field. A child whose parent has the value (a sub-feature released with its parent) is covered only where the formulas and views reading the field look at the parent too: say which. `inactiveUsers`, records whose person is deactivated, are as good as empty: an owner who left can't act on them. Important when open records lack it, otherwise Tidy-up; nothing when only finished records do.
- **Text that repeats a few values**: a `text` field with at most 15 distinct values (the facts list them with their counts), filled in a third of the records or more, should be a select. Tidy-up.
- **Dormant**: no new record (`made`, the second date) for 6 months in a database meant to keep getting them, in a Space otherwise in use. Reference lists (offices, products, categories), people lists (new records come with hires) and logs of yearly events (retreats, annual reviews) pause by nature: skip them. Tidy-up.

## A number by hand

When a finding needs a number the facts don't have, one direct `query` settles it. Never count empty values with `["=", [...], true]`: inside a count it returns the database total. Count the filled records with `false` in `q/where` of `{"q/from": "Space/Database", "q/select": ["q/count", "fibery/id"]}` and subtract: text `["=", ["q/null-or-empty?", ["Space/Field"]], false]`; number or date `["=", ["q/null?", ["Space/Field"]], false]`; relation, select or collection `["=", ["q/null?", ["Space/Field", "fibery/id"]], false]`; rich text `["=", ["q/null-or-empty?", ["Space/Description", "Collaboration~Documents/Snippet"]], false]`; checkbox `["=", ["Space/Flag"], true]`, which counts ticked records. To count the empty ones among some records (open ones, say), count those records, then the filled ones among them with both conditions in `q/and`, and subtract. A date range compares its end: `["q/end", ["Space/Dates"]]`.
